Are we running LLaDA correctly?

The official sampler reproduced 20 of the 20 previous readable answers at identical settings.

Official sampling produced 0/20 valid tours under the primary parser, and 0/20 complete tours when unambiguous bare comma lists are inspected separately. This diagnostic does not repair or rescore prior answers.

These results support correct inference for the tested configuration. All 20 complete token arrays match the official sampler. Together, these checks point to a task/prompt limitation in the TSP10 results.

Implementation and interpretation limits

Our sampler ranks confidence within the active block; the official sampler ranks a full sequence whose ineligible positions have negative-infinite confidence. These are equivalent when rankings are unique, but equal-confidence ties can select a different order. A synthetic test demonstrates this possibility. The table above records what happened with the actual model.

The earlier benchmark used two newly committed positions per step. The official chat example uses one per step and 32-position blocks. Both schedules are supported; the earlier setup was not the official chat default. Increasing output length and steps together left the same two-positions-per-step ratio. Already committed tokens are not revised by either sampler.

Repeated numerical city sequences are consistent with a weak coordinate-to-route strategy under this prompt. These tests do not prove that every prompt or decoding setting will fail, and they do not establish a fine-tuned model's capability. The three chat-default cases are diagnostic, not a new 20-instance estimate. Weight files were checked for their expected content-addressed cache names and sizes; this audit did not rehash all 16 GB of weights.

All 20 TSP10 outputs contain ten entries including the repeated starting city. A complete closed tour needs eleven entries. This is consistent with a counting or instruction-following failure; it is a behavioral interpretation, not a measurement of the model's internal reasoning. The generation procedure does not enforce the permutation constraint. A useful next prompt check would explicitly request eleven entries and allow reasoning before the final route, while keeping the official sampler fixed.
