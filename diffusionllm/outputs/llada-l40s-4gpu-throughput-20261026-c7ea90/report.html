Four-GPU L40S fine-tuning speed

26 October 2026 · nlp-l40-1 · rank 64 / alpha 64 · standard masked diffusion loss

3.73× one GPU · 1.90× two GPUs

Four GPUs complete an effective-batch-16 update in 1.157 seconds. One- and two-GPU controls were remeasured before and after the four-GPU tests, with the same source, data and training recipe.

Each reported setting has two independent runs of 24 timed updates after 3 warmups. Repeat spread is the difference between the two means divided by their pooled mean. Four-GPU reserved-memory headroom is at least 16.37 GiB per card during confirmation. The longest-sequence check also passed.

The 160k projection assumes one epoch: ceil(160000 / 16) × mean seconds per update. Loading, queueing, evaluation, checkpoint writes and preemption are additional. Four GPUs use 1.051× the estimated GPU-hours of two GPUs for the same training work.

Method and scope

The preserved workload is frozen BF16 LLaDA-8B-Instruct with FP32 rank-64 adapters in 224 projections, alpha 64, dropout 0.05, AdamW at 0.0001, weight decay 0.01, and the original response-only masked diffusion objective. The same hashed 512-example training sample spans 959–991 tokens, with mean 975.53; no truncation is used.

Six four-GPU settings were screened: microbatch 1 or 2 without checkpointing, microbatch 2 with every-fourth-layer checkpointing, and microbatch 4 with half, full, or no checkpointing. The fastest two safe candidates received longer independent confirmations in reverse order. Selection requires at least 3 GiB of reserved-memory headroom. Effectively tied candidates prefer more headroom. The selected configuration was tested on the longest 16 sampled examples.

Complete synchronized timings include forward, loss, backward, gradient reduction, clipping, optimizer, and scheduler. The reported duration is the slowest rank. Loading, JSON writes and metric-reduction calls are outside the timed region. Gradients are accumulated locally and averaged once before clipping; see the pinned PyTorch DDP implementation.

The four-process correctness gate matched a deterministic full-batch reference within floating-point rounding. Every successful real trial checked finite losses and gradients, nonzero adapter updates, frozen base samples, distinct assigned GPUs, and identical full adapter hashes across all replicas.

The actual GPU interconnect is saved in the topology record. The availability check placed three GPUs behind one PCIe switch and the fourth across CPU sockets. Results include this placement; they do not establish the speed of another GPU arrangement. Short profiles measure execution, not convergence or final TSP quality. Different layouts consume random numbers and pad batches differently.
