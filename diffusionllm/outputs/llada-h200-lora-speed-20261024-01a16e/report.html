What the additional memory changes

The H200 exposes about 139.8 GiB to CUDA versus 47.5 GiB on the L40S. Larger microbatches can fit with less activation recomputation, but memory capacity alone does not predict throughput. The measured table includes feasible, unsafe, and failed candidates.

Method and limitations

Screening: 3 warmups + 7 timed updates. Confirmation: 3 warmups + 24 timed updates, two fresh processes for each finalist in alternating order. The selected configurations also run on the longest sampled examples. All timings synchronize CUDA and cover the complete optimizer update. Setup and result-file writing are excluded. Safe candidates retain at least max(6 GiB, 5% of total memory) reserved-memory headroom.

  • Fastest confirmed among tested configurations, not a global optimum.
  • No full fine-tuning or model-quality evaluation; learning rate was fixed at 1e-4.
  • Capacity cohorts change effective batch and optimizer update count; validate quality before adopting.
  • Short profiles and sample-tail stress do not establish memory safety for longer sequences or sustained multi-day throughput.
  • Training-only projections exclude evaluation, checkpoint writes, loading, queueing, and preemption.
  • The H200 microbatch-2 baseline varies between screening and longer confirmation; a single tuning-only speedup is not a stable hardware guarantee.
  • Different microbatches can change stochastic draws and EOS padding; trajectories need not be identical.
